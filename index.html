Origin

From the Kikar tree to your table

Kikar, also known as Babool or Acacia, is a hardy tree that grows on dry land wherever water is close by, along rivers and streams. In season it is covered with small round yellow flowers. That nectar is the source of this honey.

  1. Kikar in bloomTrees along the river banks flower in season.
  2. Bees collect the nectarHoneybees forage on the Kikar blossoms and turn the nectar into honey in the hive.
  3. Harvested and filteredThe honey is taken from the hive and filtered to remove wax pieces and debris. Nothing else is done to it.
  4. Packed for youPacked by Shri Ganesh Apiaries and Honey Farm and delivered to your home.

FAQ

Common questions

What is Kikar honey?

Honey made by bees from the nectar of Kikar (Babool / Acacia) flowers. Beevora Kikar Honey is sourced from the forests of India.

Is anything added to it?

No. The only ingredient is natural Kikar honey: no additives, no preservatives, no sugar syrup, no colour or flavour.

What does "minimally processed" mean?

The honey is only filtered to remove wax pieces and debris from the hive, so it keeps its natural richness, aroma and nutrients.

My honey has turned thick or crystallised. Is it spoilt?

No. Natural honey can crystallise, especially in cold weather. Place the closed jar in warm (not boiling) water for a while and it will become liquid again.

How should I store it?

Keep the lid tightly closed, away from moisture and direct sunlight, and always use a clean, dry spoon. Stored this way, honey keeps for a very long time.
